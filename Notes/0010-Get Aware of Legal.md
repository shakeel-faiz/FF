It is completely understandable to feel overwhelmed by the legal system, but learning the basics is the best way to protect your hard-earned money and stop people from taking advantage of you. You do not need a law degree to protect yourself; you just need to understand your fundamental rights and how the system works.
## Master Your Basic Rights

* Article 10 of the Constitution: Guarantees you must be told the grounds of your arrest immediately.
* Right to counsel: You have the legal right to consult a lawyer of your choice during any detention.
* No unauthorized detention: Police cannot keep you for more than 24 hours without presenting you to a magistrate.
* Private contracts: Never hand over money without a signed legal agreement on official stamp paper. [1] 

## Learn the System for Free

* Pakistan Code: Visit the official, free government website (pakistanencode.gov.pk) to read actual federal laws.
* Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC): Read sections on FIRs and arrests to know exactly what police can and cannot do.
* PLD Publishers: Look up Pakistan Legal Decisions online or in bookstores for simplified breakdowns of major court rulings.
* Legal aid portals: Explore websites of credible organizations like the Legal Aid Society (LAS) for free citizen guides.

## How to Talk to Lawyers

* Demand a written fee structure: Ask for a breakdown of appearance fees versus total case fees before paying.
* Ask for section numbers: If a lawyer claims something is illegal, ask them exactly which section and law states that.
* Get receipts: Always get a signed, written receipt for every single rupee you hand over to a legal professional.
* Verify licenses: Check the relevant Punjab Bar Council or provincial website to ensure the person is actually a registered advocate. [2] 

## Handle Police Interactions Safely

* Read the FIR: Police cannot investigate a major (cognizable) crime against you without a formal First Information Report.
* Stay calm and record: If safe, document interactions or keep a written log of dates, names, and badge numbers.
* Use official channels: If threatened with extortion, bypass the local station and file a complaint with the District Police Officer (DPO) or the Prime Minister's Performance Delivery Unit (PMDU) app. [3, 4]
